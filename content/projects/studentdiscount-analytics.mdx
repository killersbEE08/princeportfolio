Product analytics for a live web product, owned end to end — from defining what
to measure, to turning behavior data into decisions about what to build next.

## Context

StudentDiscount.tech is a content-and-offers product where growth depends on two
things: getting the right visitors in, and keeping them engaged. When I started,
there was no structured measurement — just default pageview tracking that
couldn't answer product questions.

## The problem

You can't improve what you can't see. Without a proper measurement plan, we were
guessing about where users dropped off, which content mattered, and which
acquisition channels were actually worth the spend.

## Approach

1. **Measurement plan from scratch.** I defined a GA4 event taxonomy — custom
   events, conversions, and dimensions — mapping the full journey from
   acquisition through activation and retention.
2. **Funnel & retention analysis.** I built funnels to find the biggest drop-off
   points and tracked return-visitor behavior over time.
3. **Search-informed content roadmap.** Using Search Console and keyword-demand
   data, I prioritized content by real search intent rather than guesswork.
4. **Channel economics.** I compared acquisition channels on a cost-benefit
   basis to guide where effort and budget should go.

## What I measured

- Acquisition → activation → retention funnel with clear drop-off stages.
- Session depth and return-visitor rate as engagement signals.
- Channel-level CAC vs. value to inform budget allocation.

## Results & impact

- A defined, reusable GA4 measurement framework replaced ad-hoc tracking.
- Funnel insights drove concrete changes to site architecture and content that
  improved session depth and return-visitor rate.
- Content and channel decisions became evidence-based instead of intuition-based.

## What I learned

Most analytics setups measure vanity metrics. Designing the measurement plan
*around the product decisions* — before touching a dashboard — is what turns
analytics into a growth tool.
