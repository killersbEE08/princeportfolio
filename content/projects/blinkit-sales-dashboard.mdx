A complete Power BI solution built to answer one question the business actually
cared about: **where is our revenue really coming from, and what should we do
about it?**

## Context

Blinkit-style quick-commerce generates a huge amount of transactional data across
product categories, outlet types, and locations. The raw numbers were available,
but nobody could quickly answer "which outlet types are underperforming?" or
"which categories drive the most revenue in which regions?" without manually
slicing spreadsheets.

## The questions I set out to answer

- **Where is revenue concentrated** across product categories and outlet types?
- **Which outlets underperform** relative to similar ones, and by how much?
- **What item-level patterns** should drive the next merchandising decision?

## Data & tools

- **Source data:** item-level sales with attributes for category, outlet type,
  outlet size, location tier, and item visibility/weight.
- **SQL:** cleaned and shaped the raw tables — handling missing weights,
  standardizing category labels, and deriving fields before modeling.
- **Power BI + DAX:** a star-style data model with reusable measures for total
  sales, average sales, item count, and sales per outlet.

## Approach

1. **Model first.** I built a clean data model instead of loading a flat table,
   so filters (category, outlet type, location) propagate correctly across every
   visual.
2. **Reusable DAX measures.** Revenue, average selling price, and outlet
   performance were written once as measures and reused everywhere — no
   copy-pasted calculations.
3. **Decision-led layout.** I designed the report around the three questions
   above, not around "every chart I could make." Anything that didn't help a
   decision came off the canvas.

## What the dashboard shows

- Revenue by product category and by outlet type, with drill-down.
- Outlet-level performance ranked against peers of the same size/type.
- Item-level insights (top and bottom performers) to guide assortment decisions.

## Results & impact

- Turned a manual, spreadsheet-heavy reporting process into a single interactive
  view stakeholders could self-serve.
- Surfaced clear category- and outlet-level patterns that support merchandising
  and expansion decisions.

## What I learned

A dashboard full of accurate charts can still fail if nobody knows what to do
after looking at it. Starting from the **decision** — not the data — is what
makes analytics actually useful.
